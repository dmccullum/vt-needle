About this model

Each Vermont town gets an expected result before any votes are counted. That expectation is a statewide prior (Scott +40 by default, which you can change) adjusted by how the town voted relative to the state in the governor’s races of 2016, 2018, 2020 and 2022, with 2022 weighted most. Each town also gets an expected turnout, based mostly on its share of the statewide vote in the 2022 and 2018 midterms.

As towns report, we compare each one with its expected result. The gaps are pooled into a statewide swing and a swing for each county, with the county swings pulled toward the statewide one. Turnout is estimated the same way. The swings are applied to every town still out, and the model runs 2,000 simulations of the full count, adding town-level noise. The chance of winning is the share of simulations a candidate wins.

The needles jitter within the model’s uncertainty, as The Times’s needle did. The margin needle moves inside the middle 50% of outcomes. You can turn this off in Model priors.

Baseline data: OpenElections town-level returns for 2016–2020 and the Vermont Election Archive for 2022. Essex and Essex Junction are combined into one town. Live data: unofficial town results from the Vermont Secretary of State (electionresults.vermont.gov). The AP Elections API is also supported.
